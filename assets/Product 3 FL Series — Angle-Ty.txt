FAQ
Q1: When should I use an angle safety valve instead of an inline one?
A: Use the angle (FL) design where vertical space is limited or the discharge pipe runs to the side. The 90° body turns the outlet sideways, making piping simpler on packaged units and compact chillers; the valve action and set-pressure options are the same as the inline FS.
Q2: How does the SAE flare outlet seal?
A: The flare nut squeezes a 45° male cone against a matching seat, creating a metal-to-metal seal. The threads only provide clamping force — they do not carry the seal — so no PTFE tape is used on a flare joint.
Q3: What set pressures are available?
A: Five standard settings — 2.0, 2.4, 2.8, 3.1 and 3.4 MPa — are listed in the catalog, and custom set pressure can be produced against your equipment requirements. The valve is lead-sealed after setting.
Q4: Where is this valve installed?
A: On the pressure side of protected refrigeration equipment — receivers, condensers, compressors and other vessels — with the NPT inlet connected to the vessel and the SAE side outlet running to the discharge line.

FAQ
Q1: What is a G-thread outlet?
A: G denotes a parallel (straight) BSPP pipe thread common on European and export equipment, such as G1/2. Unlike tapered NPT, a parallel thread does not seal on the threads — it normally seals against a gasket or bonded washer at the fitting face.
Q2: What is the difference between G-thread and NPT?
A: NPT is a tapered thread that seals as the threads wedge together; G (BSPP) is parallel and seals on a gasket face. The two threads also differ in pitch and flank angle, so they are not interchangeable — this valve bridges them with an NPT inlet and a G1/2 outlet.
Q3: Can the set pressure be ordered to a custom value?
A: Yes. The catalog lists 2.0, 2.4, 2.8, 3.1 and 3.4 MPa standard settings, and the factory can set a customer-specified pressure. Each valve is lead-sealed after setting to prevent accidental adjustment.
Q4: What does the model code FL-A3G4 mean?
A: "FL" is the angle body, "A3" is a 3/8" NPT inlet, and "G4" is a G1/2 parallel-thread outlet. A = NPT inlet and G = G-thread outlet in the Hongsen coding.

FAQ
Q1: What is the difference between a pressure relief valve and a safety valve in HVAC?
A: Both protect equipment from overpressure. A spring-loaded safety valve typically opens rapidly to full lift for gas or refrigerant discharge, while a relief valve tends to open in proportion to the pressure rise. In practice the terms overlap; the HS-2F is cataloged as a two-outlet pressure relief valve within the safety valve family.
Q2: Why does this valve have two outlets?
A: The manifold body splits the discharge flow across two parallel ports, which suits larger vessels and receiver installations that vent through twin lines. Only one inlet connection to the vessel is needed, keeping mounting simple.
Q3: What pressure ratings apply to the HS-2F?
A: Maximum working pressure is 4.2 MPa (42 bar) and maximum compression pressure is 6.3 MPa (63 bar). Select the valve so the equipment's normal operating pressure stays below its relief setting and within these ratings.
Q4: Which refrigerants and temperatures is it rated for?
A: It covers R22, R134a, R410A, R507A, R448A, R449A and R404A at medium temperatures from −40 °C to +150 °C, spanning legacy HCFC systems and common HFC/HFO refrigerants.
